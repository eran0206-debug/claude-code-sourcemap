## Access and permissions

Clear every item at least five days before the game. The two that take longest are the league footage rules and locker room access.

- [ ] Written permission from the club to film Ahmad on game day inside the venue
- [ ] Media accreditation or wristbands for two crew
- [ ] Baseline or courtside position for one camera during the game (confirm which end and whether we can switch at halftime)
- [ ] Access to the tunnel and the hallway outside the locker room, before and after
- [ ] Locker room access before the game (nice to have; if refused, we shoot the hallway and the tunnel)
- [ ] League or broadcaster rules on using game footage in a personal video: what we can shoot ourselves, what we can use, and any watermark or credit requirements
- [ ] Quick verbal consent from teammates and staff who will appear in close shots; a one-line message to the team group is enough
- [ ] Opponent and officials: no close-ups of them in a way that makes them part of the story
- [ ] Music: licensed tracks only (Artlist, Epidemic, or a composer). No trending audio in the YouTube film
- [ ] Ahmad's own approval of the final cut before anything is posted
- [ ] Second location: permission from the venue if it is a restaurant or café, and a quiet hour agreed with the owner

## Crew and roles

Two people can shoot this film. Three makes the game and the sound much better. Never more than three; the whole point is that the crew disappears.

| Role | Who | On the day |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Director and A-cam | TBD | Runs the day, shoots arrival and pre-game on the gimbal, shoots the game on the long lens from the baseline, leads the conversation off camera |
| B-cam and sound | TBD | Shoots wides and details, runs the lav and boom, records room tone, holds the second angle in the conversation, captures vertical for the Reel |
| Stills and third angle (optional) | TBD | Shoots photographs through the day, holds a high wide during the game for crowd and scoreboard cut-aways |

**Ahmad's side.** One contact at the club who can open doors on the night. Ask Ahmad whether a friend or family member will be there; if so, they can be a natural interaction in beat 1.

## The conversation setup

This is the most important forty minutes of the shoot. It is a minute and a half of the film on camera and the voice-over for everything else.

**Location.** Ahmad chooses from: the car ride home, a late-night food spot in Ramat Gan, his apartment, a neighborhood court at night. Scout it or at least see photos two days before. We need: low ambient noise, a background with depth, and warm practical light we can build on.

**Cameras.** A on a tripod, tight, 50mm, eye line just off the lens on the interviewer's side. B on a tripod, wide, 35mm, showing the location. Both at 24 or 25 fps, both rolling the whole time, timecode or a clap at the start for sync.

**Light.** One soft bi-color LED as a key from the interviewer's side, matched to the room's practicals. Nothing on the background we did not find there.

**Sound.** Lav on Ahmad plus a boom just out of frame. Record a full minute of room tone before and after. In the car: engine on for the drive, engine off for the key questions, windows up.

**Running it.**

1. Five minutes of normal talk before anyone rolls. Food, the game, anything.
2. Two asks of Ahmad: full sentences, and put the question in the answer.
3. Roll both cameras and never stop. No slating between questions.
4. Follow the topic table in the Full script, beat 5. One question at a time, then "why" and "give me an example."
5. Let silence sit. Count to five in your head before the next question.
6. Close with the ending line, said to the audience, two or three versions, each shorter.
7. Keep rolling for two minutes after you say you are done. The best line often comes then.

**Interviewer.** Off camera, never heard in the final film, so never talk over him and never react out loud mid-answer. Nod, smile, wait.

## Vertical capture for the Reel

Shot natively in 9:16 on a phone with a small gimbal or on Camera B turned vertical. Grab these alongside the main coverage, never instead of it.

- [ ] Walk-in from behind, low, following him through the doors
- [ ] Tight on his face in the tunnel before the game, three seconds minimum, for the hook
- [ ] Shoes and headphones detail on the move
- [ ] Shooting routine from directly behind the basket, slow motion
- [ ] The quiet moment before tip-off, tight
- [ ] Crowd reaction, vertical, from the baseline looking up into the stands
- [ ] Bench reaction after a big play
- [ ] Post-game walk off the floor
- [ ] Conversation: a vertical angle from the side, tight, for the personality section
- [ ] Walk-out through the doors into the night

Game action itself is reframed from the 16:9 long-lens footage; a vertical camera cannot follow the play.

## Post-production plan

| Step | What happens | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Backup | Every card copied to two drives. Nobody sleeps before this is done. | Shoot night |
| Transcript | The beat 4 and beat 5 recordings transcribed. Candidate voice-over lines marked by beat. | Day 1 |
| Paper edit | The film laid out on paper: which line goes over which picture, the signature moment chosen. | Day 2 |
| Assembly | Full timeline assembled, natural sound first, music placed, no color. | Day 3 to 5 |
| Rough cut to Ahmad and client | Watermarked link. Notes in one round, within 48 hours. | Day 7 |
| Picture lock | Notes applied, runtime final. | Day 9 |
| Sound mix and color | Dialogue cleaned, music ducked under voice, color graded to the look rules. | Day 10 to 12 |
| Reel and teaser | Cut from the locked film and the vertical captures. Captions added. | Day 11 to 13 |
| Final delivery | Film in 4K 16:9, Reel and teaser in 9:16, 8 to 10 graded stills, thumbnail options. | Day 14 |
| Release order | Teaser the day before. Film and Reel the same day, film first. | Agreed with Ahmad |

**Thumbnail.** His face, mid-emotion, from the tunnel or the signature moment. His name, nothing else. Three options for him to pick from.

## Risks and fallbacks

| Risk | Fallback |
| --- | --- |
| No locker room access | Shoot the hallway, the tunnel and the kit bag. The quiet moment moves to the bench. |
| No baseline position | Shoot the game from the first row of the stands on the 70-200. Lose the low angles, keep the plays. |
| Loss, or a quiet night for Ahmad | Follow the Win or lose table in the Full script. The conversation grows, the game shrinks. |
| Limited minutes or injury | Beat 3 becomes 45 seconds of bench, crowd and one moment of effort. Beat 5 carries the film. |
| Ahmad is tight on camera | Shoot the conversation in the car or while walking. Movement loosens people. Ask about his family first. |
| Second location falls through | The conversation happens in the empty arena stands after the game, lights half down. It is a different film, still a good one. |
| League rules block game footage | We use only what we shot ourselves, and we lean on reactions, bench and crowd, which are always ours. |
| Bad arena light or flicker | Shutter at 1/50 or 1/60, test for flicker in warm-ups, manual white balance. |
| Audio lost on a key answer | Camera scratch audio from both bodies plus the recorder; we always have three sources. |
| Running long on the day | Protect the Must shots in order: beat 5, beat 4, beat 3 signature moment, beat 1 walk-in. Everything else is nice to have. |
